01 · Question and answer

Closer and farther are not opposites: both leave DDR's narrow spot on one cost frontier.

Why are tighter memory–compute coupling (A) and memory disaggregation / far memory (B) both attractive?
Answer
1

Distance is not what is optimized.

2

A answers the bandwidth pressure: more GB/s per GB, fewer pJ/bit (HBM, 3D DRAM–logic stacking, PIM).

B answers capacity cost: cheaper, extended or shared GB (compression, CXL, RDMA pools).

3

Skew is within each workload, so mixed tiers win; the DDR middle persists.

4

The directions are coupled, and since ~2010 slower $/bit declines no longer let the middle outrun both.
