06 · Skew and placement

Access is skewed within each workload, so a mix of tiers beats any single tier.

A small hot head of the bytes carries most of the traffic; the tail stays cold for minutes.

Each point restates one published statistic as (share of bytes, share of activity). Metrics and windows differ; do not compare points with each other. [D]

ρ (access density) = GB/s demanded per GB stored: the demand-side twin of slide 5's axis. I = FLOP per byte moved · MLP = memory-level parallelism.

Low arithmetic intensity alone does not decide placement: embedding gathers are 0.25 FLOP/B, yet the big tables have low bandwidth demand.

Mixing pays: production moves ~20–25% of DRAM to cheaper tiers (zswap, TMTS's Optane, Vistara's CXL) at <5% slowdown; TPP: 33% in emulation; local DRAM keeps 50–75%.
