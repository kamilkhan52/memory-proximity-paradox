03 · Pressure 1: bandwidth → A

Bandwidth must cross a boundary; each step closer adds wires, cuts pJ/bit, until another limit binds.

Bandwidth = wires crossing a boundary × rate per wire. Memory power = bandwidth × energy per bit.

Compute outgrows DRAM bandwidth ~1.9× per 2 years [D: 3.0/1.6].

Fitted rates as stated by Gholami et al.; lines, not data points.

At most ~6 HBM4 stacks fit a reticle die's two long edges.

To scale, after Samsung HC'26 s9–s11. More dies → bigger interposer (size, warpage limits).

Replotted from d-Matrix HC'26 s6 (±5% read). HBM = 8-stack package; DDR/LPDDR ≈ one 64-bit channel; GDDR6 = 384-bit bus.
d-Matrix labels HBM4's 2–3 pJ/bit "I/O", "data-movement" and "system" on different slides; its HBM2 point (≈5.6 pJ/bit) uses its own, different scope. Raptor 0.37 = measured I/O only; zHBM = Samsung 4-stack projection (HBM points 8-stack): 0.49 = total DRAM power ÷ peak BW, utilisation unstated, so a lower bound; Samsung's HBM4E baseline ≈2.0.

After the interposer, most energy sits in the DRAM core (O'Connor's HBM2 model: 3.45 of 3.92 pJ/bit, full access, modelled; newer generations lower it). 3D stacking removes the edge limit, but HBM2 I/O is already 0.3–0.8 pJ/bit by toggle rate (interposer-wire term only), vs Raptor's 0.37: core energy binds next (→ PIM, slide 7).
